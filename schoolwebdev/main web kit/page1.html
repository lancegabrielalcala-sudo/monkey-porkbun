Social Issues Essay

Understanding Unemployment

Introduction

Millions of Filipinos wake up every day with the thought, “Where will we make money to feed ourselves and our families?” Many of our fellow countrymen struggle to earn enough to afford necessities like food and shelter. This isn't because they're lazy or unskilled; it's because there aren't enough jobs to cover the bills. Because of the lack of employment, many even well-educated graduates find it difficult to find jobs that align with the courses they took in university; many are forced to take low-paying jobs that have nothing to do with what they studied. This is because they need income to survive. According to the Philippine Statistics Authority(PSA), the unemployment rate as of September 2026 stood at 5.4% which translates to 2.86 million Filipinos having no jobs, while the underemployment rate stood at 11.0% representing 5.56 million workers seeking extra hours of work or looking for better-paying employment. They take whatever work they can find because their current livelihood simply cannot support their families' basic needs. These are not just numbers or statistics but they stand for millions of Filipinos who struggle for so little money. Seeking the truth of our employment crisis is the first step toward justice for every Filipino who deserves a fair chance at a better and more secure life. Unemployment is not just a money or economic issue; it is also a moral concern that challenges human dignity The source of this crisis is due to several factors such as mismatched education and skills, slow industrial growth, and limited local opportunities. In 2026, 592,000 Filipinos aged 15 to 24 joined the workforce, yet only 310,000 found jobs; meaning nearly half remain unemployed. As a result, many Filipino workers are forced to leave the country for higher pay and better opportunities because our own economy cannot sustain them. Presently, the International Labor Organization states that more than 1 million Filipinos each year leave the country to work abroad. This essay argues that the Philippine government must prioritize job creation through industrial development, strengthen support for local businesses, and reform our education system so graduates are prepared for in-demand careers, because steady and sustainable employment is not only essential for economic growth but also for upholding truth, justice, human dignity, and moral responsibility in our nation. We cannot build a better country when so many people are left behind, struggling to survive. The government must take action to ensure that every Filipino has access to decent work and a fair chance at a better life.

Firstly, we need to face the truth; our education system is not creating students who meet the skills demanded by many industries and sectors. Many young Filipinos complete their schooling ready to enter the workforce but end up unemployed due to the fact that their education did not instill the skills that are in demand for many occupations. The outdated education system in the Philippines has many graduates who are overqualified in terms of academics, many of whom finish school with a four-year bachelor's degree, sometimes even completing with a master’s degree. ut in terms of practical skills such as critical thinking, technical skills which include data analysis and advanced software proficiency, and soft skills like workplace communication; countless students simply lack the needed requirements for the ever-changing job market. According to Professor Emily Christi Cabegin from the School of Labor and Industrial Relations of the University of the Philippines, “In this era, where technological change is constant, we must foster a culture of continuous learning and skills development “. This statement alone highlights the need for new developments in our schooling system to catch up in this era.
