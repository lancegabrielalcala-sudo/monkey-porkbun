When work is scarce here in our country many Filipinos are left with a choice: Stay in the country where their loved ones are but struggle or sacrifice themselves to go abroad and work to provide for their families. Many choose to leave for a better life because our country couldn't give them dignified work. Reports say that over 1 million OFWs leave per year for various countries. Though they may earn better wages, it is at the cost of being thousands of miles away from their families. This sad reality reminds us why providing good jobs is not just an economic goal but a moral duty that protects the dignity of Filipinos and the unity of these families. Secondly, beyond education reform, we must also create more stable and decent jobs here at home; because every Filipino deserves to do meaningful work. When a person does not have work, it often hits their sense of worth and purpose. Many face judgment from others since unemployment is often perceived as a failure or laziness by many; this repeatedly makes them believe that their efforts and hard work were not valuable since they could not secure work. Moreover, we can resolve this problem by supporting the local businesses already here in the Philippines. As dictated by the United Nations Development Programme, micro, small, and medium businesses make up 99.5% of all business establishments in the nation, providing 63% of employment in the workforce, and contributing a massive 40% to the country’s Gross Domestic Product(GDP). The problem is that many of these smaller businesses are most likely to face challenges such as high costs just for business permits, taxes, and compliance which take an enormous amount of money from their already limited budget. Instead of that money going back to the business to expand and improve, many businesses are stuck being small since owners are afraid to grow their businesses because it means more permits and taxes that take money and time. Moreover, this also prevents both foreign and local investors from investing since they see how complicated and risky it is to start a business here in the Philippines and they are most likely to choose to avoid it. Furthermore, since many of these industries remain small, this affects the availability of job opportunities for the hundreds of thousands of young people who enter the workforce to find employment. As stated by the Congressional Policy and Budget Research Department (CPBRD) of the House of Representatives, approximately 796,000 students graduate from higher education institutions each year roughly 700,000 to 800,000 annually yet only about 400,000 decent jobs are created to absorb them. This translates to a significant portion of our Filipino graduates being unemployed due to the lack of available occupations in our economy. To resolve this problem, the government should impose longer tax holidays, which allow MSME(Micro, Small, Medium Enterprises) businesses to keep the profits from sales; hence it results in the business having a better chance to flourish during the first risky years of operation where a business is most likely to close down due to lack of sales or limited budget. This longer tax holiday proposal will allow these smaller enterprises to reinvest which will lead to growth; then it will provide more employment and opportunities to countless people. Additionally, another solution is to decrease the income tax for MSMEs to 5%; hence during difficult times when income is low, the additional money from lower taxes will serve as a buffer to assist the business in surviving. By supporting local businesses we increase the availability of employment and opportunities for numerous people. When we lower barriers for these businesses, not only do we help them economically; we also help uphold the dignity of all Filipinos. It becomes easier to find good jobs and fair opportunities at home without leaving your family.

Some may argue that Filipinos seeking work and opportunities in other countries is positive because OFWs send remittances home, which help boost the Philippine economy. But this idea dismisses the fact that many of these OFWs spend years being separated from their families because they have no choice. After all, the jobs here do not pay enough for their daily needs and many of them suffer emotionally due to this separation. Furthermore, remittances help in the short term by boosting the economy and improving the quality of life for the families of these OFWs. But in the long term, it has multiple problems namely: our country being dependent on people leaving to make money abroad instead of creating jobs here; it creates a false sense of progress since it means there are not enough decent jobs in the country; and lastly “brain drain” where our most skilled and well-educated people leave to work elsewhere which translates to our country losing the very same people that might help build it. In our argument, we advocate for change in our country itself since not only is it better in the long term but it means every Filipino can have a good and stable life here not overseas where they are separated from their loved ones. In conclusion, the unemployment crisis in the Philippines is more than just an economic problem; it is a test of our nation’s commitment to truth, justice, human dignity, and moral responsibility. We cannot claim to be a progressing country when millions of our countrymen are forced to choose between feeding their families and being with them. Reforming our education, supporting local businesses, and removing barriers to decent work are not just policy choices; they are moral duties to ensure every Filipino gets to have a decent life here. True progress is not measured by how many people succeed abroad but by how many of our people can build dignified, prosperous lives right here in their homeland. It is time to build a country where no one should have to leave just to survive and provide, because every Filipino deserves to have a future at home.
